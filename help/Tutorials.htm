Seven short walks, each a real job done from start to finish, each about three minutes. Take the overview and walk one first; the rest go in any order.

Simulations, made with AI. One synthetic voice does the work; a second answers as a screen reader would. The screen reader’s lines are written the way a reader speaks at its middle verbosity setting – the name, the kind of control, its state, and the access key – so what you hear in the walk is what you will hear on your own machine. You always know which voice is talking.

Every key is named in full the first time it appears, with the word it comes from: Alt plus T is Transcribe, T for Transcribe.

How to listen

The audio is in help\tutorials, one .mp3 per walk, named as the walks are numbered. Open the folder and play the one you want in any media player. In FileDir, put the cursor on Tutorials.m3u there and press Control+Shift+H for the Homer Player: it lists all seven as tracks, and Control+Page Down and Control+Page Up move between them.

The written version of every walk follows below, and TutorialFeed.xml beside this file is a podcast feed of the same audio.

If a recording is full of names it gets wrong, add them to the vocabulary setting in the configuration, separated by commas.
