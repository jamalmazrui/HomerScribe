WHY THESE ARE THE INTERESTING TEST

Everything tested so far has been 78 to 86 percent speech, where there is no

room for description and most moments fall on somebody talking. A silent film

is the other end of the scale: no speech at all, so every gap is a real gap and

every description lands in silence. Placement should be near perfect, and for

once the measurement will be of the DESCRIPTIONS rather than of where they

were put.

Two things worth watching in the results.

Intertitles. A silent film carries its dialogue on printed cards. Those are

text on screen, which the vision model reads and speaks aloud – so HomerScribe

turns an inaccessible film into an accessible one in the most literal sense

available. This is the case where it should look best.

The score. There is usually music throughout, and Whisper hallucinates on

music: it invents speech that is not there. The loop filter catches a sentence

repeated back to back, and the “hardly any speech was heard” check now catches

a near-empty transcript. A silent film will test both harder than anything so

far. Expect the transcript to be nonsense or empty, and read the log for what

the speech detection reported.

Buster Keaton, The General (1926), 79 minutes

Public domain in the United States since 1954, the copyright not having been

renewed. On the National Film Registry. Orson Welles called it the greatest

comedy ever made. A locomotive chase through the Civil War: almost continuous

physical action, which is the hardest thing description has to do and the most

worth doing.

On The Birth of a Nation (1915)

It is public domain, it is on the National Film Registry, and it is studied

seriously in film history for two reasons at once: it invented much of the

grammar of narrative cinema, and it is a work of racist propaganda that was

used to recruit for the Ku Klux Klan. Both facts are why it is studied.

You are right that this is information you have as much right to as anyone.

I did not put it at the top of the file for a different reason: as a test it

is poor. At over three hours it is the longest thing you would have run, and

what HomerScribe would tell you about it is what is happening in each shot,

which is not what makes the film worth examining. The General gives you the

same silent-film test in half the time.

If you do want it, it is on the Internet Archive under its title, and I would

rather find you a confirmed address than have you search for it among the

reuploads.
