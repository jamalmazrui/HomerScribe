W3C WAI “Web Accessibility Perspectives” videos, for testing HomerScribe.

Ten short films from the W3C Web Accessibility Initiative, about 48 seconds

each. Each has an official audio-described version made by the W3C, and each

topic page publishes a text transcript with a description of the visuals, in

two columns. Those published descriptions are the point: they let HomerScribe’s

output be compared line by line against description written by professionals,

rather than only judged by ear.

The urls below are the UNDESCRIBED versions, which is what HomerScribe should

be given. Compare its results against the descriptions on the pages listed at

the bottom of this file.

W3C material is permissively licensed, so the comparison may be quoted.

Lines beginning with # are notes and are ignored.

The ten films individually.

I could NOT confirm the individual YouTube addresses from here, so they are

not listed: a wrong address would waste a run. To add them, open each page

below, take the address of the video it plays, and paste it in as its own

line. The pages are confirmed.

1. Keyboard Compatibility w3.org/WAI/perspective-videos/keyboard/

2. Colors with Good Contrast w3.org/WAI/perspective-videos/contrast/

3. Clear Layout and Design w3.org/WAI/perspective-videos/layout/

4. Text to Speech w3.org/WAI/perspective-videos/speech/

5. Large Links, Buttons, Controls w3.org/WAI/perspective-videos/controls/

6. Video Captions w3.org/WAI/perspective-videos/captions/

7. Customizable Text w3.org/WAI/perspective-videos/customizable/

8. Speech Recognition w3.org/WAI/perspective-videos/voice/

9. Understandable Content w3.org/WAI/perspective-videos/understandable/

10. Notifications and Feedback w3.org/WAI/perspective-videos/notifications/

Of the ten, “Video Captions” is the single best test: continuous narration for

transcribing, and visual-only information the narration never states – a

hearing aid, and a library revealed only at the end – which is exactly what

describing is for.
