Science video sources for HomerScribe

This file is both a note to read and a source file to give HomerScribe: lines

beginning with # are ignored, so everything explanatory here is skipped and

only the addresses are acted on.

Every address below was opened and confirmed. Nothing is here on the strength

of a search result alone.

As it stands, ONE video is active: a feature-length NASA documentary. The

playlists are commented out with their sizes, so you can uncomment whichever

you mean rather than starting a run of a hundred films by accident.

Cosmic Dawn: The Untold Story of the James Webb Space Telescope.

NASA+ original, June 2025, 1 hour 37 minutes. Public domain as US government

work, so anything HomerScribe makes from it can be shared freely. Visually

dense – mirror segments, clean rooms, test chambers, deployment animations –

with interviews throughout to transcribe. The best single test I found.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uSMGENDH_QI

NOVA – the PBS series you were thinking of

—————————————————————————

NOVA has run on PBS since 1974 and is the most watched science series on

American television. Its own channel publishes complete documentaries, not

merely clips.

Full Documentaries, NOVA PBS Official, 106 videos, hour-long:

https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLtiOgIRVt4053SXtk0cVIIkhFfbfkac0e

A hundred and six hour-long films is days of work. Open the playlist, pick

three or four, and paste their addresses here instead.

Worth knowing: PBS publishes audio-described versions of many NOVA and NATURE

programmes on pbs.org, professionally written and performed. Describing a NOVA

episode with HomerScribe and comparing it against PBS’s own description of

another would be the most honest demonstration you could give.

NASA

—————————————————————————

ScienceCasts, NASA Science, 332 videos of a few minutes each:

https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PL8F7BC3F1240213F5

Good material – the descriptions read out diagram labels and schematics that

the narration skips over – but 332 is far too many, and each is short enough

that the setup costs as much as the film.

NASA’s long-form documentaries are published on NASA+ at plus.nasa.gov and put

on YouTube as individual videos rather than gathered into playlists, which is

why there is no playlist here to point at. Cosmic Dawn above is one of them.

The universities, and why none are listed

—————————————————————————

I could not confirm a specific address for any of the institutions you named,

and I would rather say so than list addresses I have not opened.

But there is a better reason to leave them out. What universities publish

freely is lecture recordings – MIT OpenCourseWare alone has over 7,000 videos,

the largest .edu channel on YouTube. A lecture is a person at a lectern beside

a slide. HomerScribe would transcribe it well, and would have almost nothing

worth describing: the picture carries the speaker and the slide, and the slide

is usually read aloud anyway.

The material where description earns its place is the opposite: something

happening on screen that the sound does not account for. NOVA and NASA are

that. Lectures are not.

If you want a university source anyway, the Smithsonian Institution Archives

published a Science Media Group collection – Smithsonian Astrophysical

Observatory and Harvard College Observatory, 1989 to 2013 – as a YouTube

playlist. I did not confirm its address and have not listed it.
