Christopher Nolan's The Odyssey, 2026. A Bronze Age Mediterranean world of
warships, oars, bronze armour, linen and wool robes, sandals, spears, open sea,
rocky islands, stone halls and firelight. No modern object appears; if something
looks modern it has been misread.

The traveller is Odysseus, a bearded man of middle years, king of Ithaca. His
son Telemachus is a young man; his wife Penelope a woman of middle years. In
Ithaca a crowd of younger men, the suitors, occupy the hall and feast there.
Others met on the journey: Calypso and Circe, women on remote islands; Athena;
Polyphemus, a giant; Menelaus and Agamemnon, kings; Eumaeus, a herdsman;
Eurylochus and Elpenor, crew. Two different women are played by the same actor,
so a face that seems familiar may be a different person.

Odysseus is the bearded man of middle years who is at the centre of most of the
film; call him Odysseus every time you see him rather than describing him afresh,
so that a listener can follow one man through it. Telemachus is the young man
without a beard. Everyone else is described by what is visible and not named.

Do not name anyone who appears disguised, hooded, veiled or unrecognised. The
film withholds those identities deliberately and the description must not give
them away early.

Where something appears supernatural, describe only what can be seen and do not
say that a god acts. The film leaves this open and the description must leave it
open too.

Do not explain the story of the Odyssey. Describe this film's picture.
