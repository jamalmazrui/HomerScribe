The Odyssey — context for HomerDescribe

You are writing audio description for Christopher Nolan’s 2026 film The Odyssey, adapted from Homer. It was the first feature shot entirely on IMAX 70mm film, made with practical effects, real locations and no green screen, so what you see is physically there. The world is late Bronze Age Greece, researched from fragmentary archaeology: timber ships with banks of oars and square sails, bronze armour and plumed helmets, spears and round shields, stone halls with open hearths, olive groves, cliffs, desert dunes and open sea. Costume is linen, leather and wool in undyed and earth colours, with the texture of hard wear. Agamemnon’s armour is deliberately richer than anyone else’s, blackened bronze, silver and sulphur, and it hides almost all of him. Light and weather carry the mood: hard sun, storm, torchlight, firelit dark. The film opens on the words “In a time of apparent magic” and is told partly out of order.

CHARACTERS. Odysseus, played by Matt Damon, king of Ithaca, a weathered bearded man, often salt-stained, in a cloak or battle gear, sometimes drawing a great bow; the central figure in almost every scene of the voyage. Penelope, played by Anne Hathaway, his wife, queen of Ithaca, holding off the suitors, often in the palace hall. Telemachus, played by Tom Holland, their grown son, prince of Ithaca, searching for his father. Athena, played by Zendaya, goddess of wisdom and war, appearing to Odysseus; Zendaya also plays the young Trojan priestess killed in the sack of Troy, whose form Athena later takes. Antinous, played by Robert Pattinson, the sleek, cruel leader of the suitors. Eumaeus, played by John Leguizamo, the half-blind swineherd, Odysseus’s faithful old servant. Eurylochus, played by Himesh Patel, Odysseus’s second in command, who questions him. Calypso, played by Charlize Theron, the nymph of Ogygia who drugs Odysseus with lotus. Circe, played by Samantha Morton, the witch of Aeaea who turns men into pigs. Polyphemus, the Cyclops, son of Poseidon, performed by Bill Irwin as an animatronic puppet designed after Goya’s Saturn Devouring His Son, seen mostly as vast arms out of cave darkness. Sinon, played by Elliot Page, a young Greek soldier, decoy of the Trojan Horse, who dies and returns as a shade. Menelaus, played by Jon Bernthal, king of Sparta. Helen and her twin Clytemnestra, both played by Lupita Nyong’o. Agamemnon, played by Benny Safdie, king of Mycenae. Mentor, played by Ryan Hurst, Telemachus’s guide. Tiresias, played by James Remar, the blind prophet in Hades. Melantho and Melanthius, played by Mia Goth and Logan Marshall-Green, disloyal twin servants. Argos is Odysseus’s old dog.

STORY, so you can recognise where you are. Ithaca has been twenty years without its king. Suitors have taken the palace and press Penelope to remarry. Telemachus sails with Mentor to Sparta, to Menelaus and Helen, seeking news; Antinous plots to kill him on his return. On Ogygia, Athena appears to Odysseus, whom Calypso has kept seven years and dulled with lotus, and his past comes back: the Greek army under Agamemnon taking Troy by the Trojan Horse with Sinon as decoy; the cave of Polyphemus, who eats several men before they blind him and escape; the giant Laestrygonians killing most of the crew; Circe on Aeaea; the crossing of Oceanus into Hades, where Odysseus meets the shades of Tiresias, Sinon and Agamemnon; the Sirens, Charybdis and Scylla; the cattle of the Sun on Thrinacia, whose slaughter brings the storm that sinks the ship and leaves Odysseus alone. Freed at last, he rafts to Ithaca, is taken in by Eumaeus, saves Telemachus from the bandits, and is recognised by his dying dog Argos. Disguised, he endures Antinous at the feast and confesses his guilt to Penelope. She sets the contest of the great bow and the row of axe heads; only he can string it; the hall becomes a battle and the suitors are killed. He gives the throne to Telemachus and sails west with Penelope.

Running through it: Zeus’s law of hospitality between guest and host, the dread of the “people from the sea”, and Odysseus’s guilt for the men he lost.
