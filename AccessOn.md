I have listened to every episode of Access On, and to a good deal of what came before it, so it is about time I wrote in. I am a member of the Seattle chapter of the National Federation of the Blind.

I have released a free, open source Windows program called HomerScribe. It describes what happens on screen in a video and writes down what is said in it, and it does both on the user's own computer.

Three things about it may interest your listeners.

The first is that nothing leaves the machine. Once it is installed there is no account, no key, and no request to anyone's server. That has a consequence beyond privacy: describing a two hour film means several hundred calls to an AI model, which through a commercial service is a great deal of usage, and here it costs nothing at all. It also means a person can describe a recording they would never upload anywhere.

The second is what comes out. Describing produces a new copy of the film with spoken description mixed into its sound, and a written script of every description with its timing. Transcribing produces the transcript. Ask for both and you get a third document with the speech and the descriptions interleaved in the order they happen, which for a deafblind reader is the whole film in one place.

The third is a limitation I would rather state than have people discover. Audio description written and performed by professionals is far better than this, and description is a craft. HomerScribe is for the very large amount of material that has no description at all, where the comparison is not with a professional describer but with silence.

Transcribing needs about half a gigabyte of models; describing needs about six and a half. The installer offers each separately.
